In early 2023 Dubai scrapped the tourist alcohol licence — until then non-residents technically needed a licence card to buy from off-licence shops. The rule change made Dubai more relaxed than Abu Dhabi, Sharjah, Ajman and other emirates. Here's what the current rules actually say.

## Buying from shops

MMI and African+Eastern now sell to tourists on presentation of passport only. No licence card, no application forms. Store staff scan the passport, log the purchase, and apply a 30% tourist/VAT discount.

## Hotel delivery

Most 4* and 5* hotels will deliver bottles to rooms for a markup (300–500% over MMI prices). Legal, convenient, pricey. Easier to buy at the airport duty-free and bring to the room.

## Drinking in licensed venues

Any venue with a Dubai Tourism alcohol licence (hotel bars, licensed restaurants, nightclubs, beach clubs). No ID check beyond age (21+). The DED tag or hotel brand is your signal — if the venue is inside a hotel or branded by one, assume licensed.

## What's still illegal

Public drinking (streets, parks, beaches outside licensed clubs). Driving with any alcohol in blood (0.00% tolerance, serious fines + possible jail). Being visibly drunk in public. Carrying open bottles outside a venue.

## Other emirates differ

Abu Dhabi and Sharjah are stricter. Sharjah is dry — no alcohol sold or served. Abu Dhabi allows it in hotels but off-licence is more restricted. Within Dubai itself the 2023 rules apply.
