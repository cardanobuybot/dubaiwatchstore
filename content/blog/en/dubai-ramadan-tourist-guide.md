Ramadan transforms Dubai for a month each year. Daytime becomes quieter and more respectful, nights come alive. Tourists aren't required to fast but should respect visible rules. Here's what actually changes.

## The daytime rules

No eating, drinking (even water) or smoking in public from sunrise to sunset. In 2023 Dubai softened some rules — many hotel restaurants now serve lunch openly, and some non-hotel cafés put up screens or partitions. On the street or in a car window, still don't eat/drink. Being caught = police warning, repeat = 1,000 AED fine.

## Iftar — breaking fast

At sunset the whole city shifts gear. Every hotel, every mall food court does iftar buffets — the sunset meal — from 18:00. Prices 80–350 AED per person depending on venue. Family-friendly, respectful, worth experiencing even as a non-Muslim.

## Suhoor — the late-night meal

The second meal before dawn, around 02:00–03:00. Shisha tents on hotel lawns, light bites, tea. Dubai's late-night scene during Ramadan is genuinely good.

## Working hours

Shorter — most offices do 09:00–15:00. Malls open later (14:00) and close later (02:00). Government offices close 15:00. Plan logistics around this.

## Nightlife and alcohol

Clubs stay open but music is softer, no live DJs on some nights (depends on venue licence). Alcohol is served after sunset in licensed venues. Hotel bars operate normally, nightclubs may close earlier (02:00 instead of 04:00).
